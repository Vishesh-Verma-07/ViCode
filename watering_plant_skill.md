# Simple Skill: Watering a Houseplant

## Overview
A quick guide to watering a typical indoor houseplant.

## What You'll Need
- A watering can or a small pitcher
- A tray or saucer (optional)
- A plant that needs water

## Steps
1. **Check the soil** – Stick your finger about an inch into the soil. If it feels dry, it’s time to water.
2. **Prepare water** – Use room‑temperature water; avoid water that is too cold or hot.
3. **Water the plant** – Slowly pour water over the soil until it starts to drain out the bottom. 
   - Aim for the soil, not the leaves, to prevent rot.
4. **Let it drain** – If you have a saucer, allow the excess water to collect there for a few minutes, then empty any remaining water so the roots don’t sit in water.
5. **Clean up** – Wipe any spills and return the plant to its usual spot.

## How Often?
- Most indoor plants need watering **once a week**, but always check the soil first. 
- In warmer or brighter spots, they may need water more frequently; in low‑light areas, less often.

## Quick Tips
- **Better underwater than overwater** – It’s easier to revive a dry plant than one with root rot.
- **Use a moisture meter** if you’re unsure; they give a reading of how dry the soil is.
- **Observe the leaves** – Yellowing or wilting can signal over‑ or under‑watering.
